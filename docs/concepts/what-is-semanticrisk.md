# What SemanticRisk Is

SemanticRisk is an evidence-led framework for AI interpretation assurance.

It examines what AI systems understand from an organisation's public website, including the claims they extract, how they classify the organisation, where models disagree, what evidence may be shaping the result, and whether that interpretation changes over time.

## Primary question

SemanticRisk is designed to answer:

> What did the AI system understand from the available public evidence, and how stable is that interpretation?

## Evidence-to-interpretation chain

SemanticRisk separates the process into five stages:

1. Capture — what public website evidence was accessible and usable.
2. Extract — which claims and classifications an AI system formed.
3. Compare — where interpretations agree, disagree, omit, narrow, or overstate.
4. Monitor — whether claims, capture conditions, or interpretation change over time.
5. Review — whether a change is material and what may explain it.

## Why this distinction matters

A generated answer can look plausible while still containing a materially misleading, narrowed, incomplete, unstable, or misplaced interpretation.

Examples include:

- placing an organisation in the wrong category;
- reducing a broad platform to one narrow capability;
- omitting a material capability;
- overstating a capability;
- attributing a capability incorrectly;
- producing materially different claims across models from the same evidence;
- changing interpretation even when the underlying website content appears stable.

## What SemanticRisk is not

SemanticRisk does not:

- determine objective truth;
- certify safety or compliance;
- guarantee control over downstream AI output;
- replace human judgement;
- act as a standalone decision-maker.

It is an interpretation and assurance framework, not an enforcement system.
