# Fictional Unified Report example

> **Demonstration only.** Every company, domain, observation and result below is synthetic. No AI provider was queried and no real organisation was assessed.

This worked example shows how a SemanticRisk Unified Report separates technical access, buyer-question visibility, interpretation accuracy, competitive displacement and compatible-cycle change.

## Executive meaning

**State: Action required**

The fictional company is technically reachable and is interpreted accurately when named, but it appears in only **9 of 40** neutral buyer-question observations. Beacon Metrics appears more often and owns most third-party citations.

**Decision:** strengthen public evidence for the two buyer tasks where Northstar is absent, then repeat the same compatible matrix before claiming improvement.

## Buyer-question visibility

| Fictional company | Mentions |
|---|---:|
| Northstar Analytics | 9 |
| Beacon Metrics | 14 |
| Orbit Signal | 6 |

A single answer may mention multiple companies. Counts do not need to total 40.

Northstar appears for technical-evaluation questions but is usually absent from procurement and governance questions. Beacon's synthetic third-party evidence is cited more consistently.

## Interpretation assessment

- **Supported: 5** — category, primary capability, deployment model, security documentation and headquarters align with the synthetic public evidence.
- **Partially supported: 2** — two capability statements are broader than the fictional product pages.
- **Contradicted: 1** — the observed answer describes Northstar as enterprise-only, while the fictional current pricing includes self-service.

Unsupported is not automatically false, and an uncited statement is not automatically a hallucination.

## Change since the compatible cycle

| Measure | Previous | Current | Observed change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Target mentions | 4 of 40 | 9 of 40 | +5 observations |
| Owned-domain citations | 1 | 3 | +2 citations |
| Supported claims | 4 of 8 | 5 of 8 | +1 supported claim |

These are associations between two compatible synthetic cycles. The example does not claim that a website edit caused the movement.

## Prioritised actions

1. **Publish an evidence-led page for the missing procurement buyer task.**
   - Why: Northstar was absent in all eight synthetic procurement-oriented observations.
   - Verify: repeat the same eight questions in the next compatible cycle.
2. **Correct the enterprise-only ambiguity across pricing, product and organisation schema.**
   - Why: one material interpretation conflicts with the synthetic current offer.
   - Verify: repeat the branded interpretation probe and preserve the answer and sources.
3. **Add omitted support pages to the sitemap and monitor discovery.**
   - Why: three relevant fictional pages are public but absent from sitemap evidence.
   - Verify: confirm sitemap inclusion, retrieval and later citation movement separately.

## Monitoring watchlist

- Does Beacon Metrics continue to dominate procurement-oriented questions?
- Does the enterprise-only description persist after the evidence is clarified?
- Do owned-domain citations increase without reducing third-party corroboration?

## Evidence boundaries

SemanticRisk reports do not promise control over downstream AI systems, knowledge of hidden training or retrieval, guaranteed recommendation or citation, causation from before-and-after association, or search-ranking, market-share or revenue outcomes.

A first observation is a baseline. Drift requires a later compatible observation. Technical readiness is not AI visibility. Presence is not recommendation, and recommendation is not proof of suitability.
